# B26 test evidence

Executed locally using Node 22.20.0; the backend requires at least 22.22.3. No system installation was changed.

- `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1`: two tests passed. The 24-hour local fixture produced 24 distinct runs: 14 no-match, nine blocked non-authoritative B22 drafts, and one settled allocation for 2,541 base units. Duplicate tick, one intent/attempt, a killed worker and a replacement process, and local reorganization were checked. A genuine two-allocation market then progressed from a 10,624-unit partial payment to 14,608 units paid using two attempts. Payable markets were pre-enrolled with synthetic policies and session context.
- `B26_TEST_HANDOFF_RACE=1` and `B26_TEST_POST_HANDOFF_RACE=1` with the same harness: two tests passed per run. An expired worker was blocked before dispatch; after a committed handoff, revocation could not retract a delayed broadcast.
- `B26_TEST_CRASH_AFTER_HANDOFF=1` and `B26_TEST_CRASH_AFTER_BROADCAST=1` with the same harness: two tests passed per run. Separate recovery processes did not restore original wallet keys or submit replacement payments.
- `tools/b26-run-postgres-tests.ps1`: disposable B26 role, idempotency, fencing, B22 and read-only preview checks passed, including authenticated control requests and denied writes.
- `tools/b21-run-postgres-tests.ps1`, `tools/b24a1-run-postgres-tests.ps1`, `tools/b24a2-run-postgres-tests.ps1`, `tools/b24-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1`, and `tools/b25-run-postgres-tests.ps1`: passed in disposable environments during this implementation. The B21 legacy fixture remained unchanged. Earlier transient startup/schema-drift and fixture failures were corrected and rerun.
- `node --test` on discovered `*.test.mjs` files in `apps/backend/scripts`, `tools`, and `packages/shared/tests`: 1,402 passed, zero failed or skipped. `.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m pytest --import-mode=importlib -p no:cacheprovider -q`: 608 passed. Backend Vitest: 25 files, 175 passed. Backend typecheck and lint passed.
- `node node_modules/prisma/build/index.js validate --config prisma7.config.ts` and `generate --config prisma7.config.ts` passed. `hardhat compile --force`: five Solidity files, solc 0.8.34. Standalone Hardhat: 90 passing and four pending database-dependent tests, which use separate harnesses.

These results do not establish every requested B26 adversarial case or uninterrupted signing of new allocations after complete signer-process loss. See `B26_ACCEPTANCE_MATRIX.md`; unexecuted checks are not passes.

Latest changes: `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-run-postgres-tests.ps1` passed after adding authenticated lease renewal; its role tests reject renewal by another worker, after expiration, and by the reporter. `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed two tests after the multi-allocation fixture was changed to discover existing reservations from PostgreSQL rather than an allocation-to-policy map. This discovery requires a separately established local session and grant and does not discover or create an unreserved authorization. `node --test apps/backend/scripts/b26-utc-hours.test.mjs` passed four tests.

Additional run: `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed two tests after adding read-only policy discovery for unreserved allocations. The genuine two-allocation fixture checks that a valid policy without an approved reservation-bound session is blocked and no reservation is inserted. It then uses separately approved fixture capabilities to complete local payments. This does not establish autonomous capability provisioning.

Recovery correction: the same wallet-bound command passed two tests after exercising a policy revoked after settlement. The worker rediscovered the existing attempt without a funding grant, reconciled through B25, and the market remained at two attempts. This does not authorize signing or submission after revocation.

Lease-check change: `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed two tests with additional lease checks before B24 signing, funding, allowance and B25 intent preparation. This run verifies the ordinary path; it does not replace the outstanding delayed-operation and process-restart tests.

Recorded-session recovery: `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed two tests after deleting a settled allocation's in-memory session and revoking its policy. B26 restored the same public session from PostgreSQL, checked the local chain anchor and runtime code, and reconciled without another attempt. This exercises loss of the session object in one process; it does not establish signer supervision or a separate-process payment runtime.

In-process supervisor: `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed two tests with the two-allocation market using `createOfflinePaymentSupervisor`. It checks the run lease and allocation membership, delegates to B24/B25 and rejects a stale worker. The fixture still supplies reservation-bound capabilities in the same process; separate-process transport, restart and new-reservation provisioning remain unverified.

Separate scheduler process: `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed two tests with a forked `b26-scheduler-cli.mjs` in `SUPERVISED_TEST` mode. The parent retained both ephemeral signers and attached the private supervisor IPC channel; the child processed the second allocation and the genuine market reached 14,608 base units paid with two attempts. Both allocations had been reserved and their sessions and grants established by the fixture. This does not test provisioning an unreserved allocation, supervisor restart, six paid hours, or the complete security matrix.

New-allocation provisioning: `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed two tests after changing the second allocation to start without a reservation. A separate scheduler process found its existing simulated policy; the trusted fixture owner supplied a preapproved buyer/deployment, anchor and cumulative funding budget; and the supervisor created one reservation, session and bounded funding grant before B24/B25 settled it. Database readback verified the grant is linked to that budget. Before the fixture approval was supplied, no reservation was created. The B24 approver remains a trusted fixture principal and has broader database privileges than this component uses. This run does not establish six paid hours, full process-restart coverage or the complete security and regression matrix.

Funding-approval negative cases: rerunning `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed two tests after adding checks for a missing fixture capability and a different buyer's funding budget. Both were rejected before the reservation was created; the correct approved budget then enabled the existing supervised settlement test.

Six-payable-hour scenario: `$env:B26_SIX_HOURS_ONLY='1'; powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed one test in 39 seconds after correcting an initial fixture SQL parameter mismatch. The unmodified B22 simulator and ingestion service produced 24 finalized historical hours. A separate supervised scheduler settled six valid exact-payment allocations, recorded 18 no-match runs, and completed a second pass in a new scheduler process with the same parent-held signer registry. Readback found six unique settlement IDs, six buyer/nonce pairs and exactly six B25 attempts; each buyer debit and seller credit matched the integer payment. This test starts from already-closed historical hours, so no real hourly wait or production clock override is used. It does not test every mid-operation crash, RPC fault or role case.

Mid-run restart correction: the initial expanded test stopped at five payments because a worker died with a snapshot checkpoint and its lease expired while the catch-up cursor scanned later hours. A second run also exposed an undrained child-output pipe in the fixture. After adding bounded due-run discovery with new fenced claims, preventing a disconnected child from renewing its lease, and draining test output, `$env:B26_SIX_HOURS_ONLY='1'; powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` passed one test in 106 seconds. The first scheduler was killed after three payments; a second process completed all 24 runs and six payments with the original parent-held wallets and exactly six attempts. `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-run-postgres-tests.ps1` passed the due-run query's authenticated-role, bounded-query and expired-lease checks. A scheduler restart is not the same as restarting the process that retains the signer registry.

Follow-up regression: the mixed-hour test initially failed because its callback advanced the test clock before the due-run scan and the next market was processed early. The scheduler now scans due work before invoking the callback. `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b26-wallet-bound-postgres.ps1` then passed two tests, and the six-hour command above passed one test in 96 seconds. The first B21 harness attempt failed during disposable PostgreSQL startup; a subsequent `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b21-run-postgres-tests.ps1` passed. The B26/B22, B24-A1, B24-A2, B24 wallet-bound and B25 disposable harnesses passed in this verification effort. These runs do not cover every required B26 crash and RPC failure case.
