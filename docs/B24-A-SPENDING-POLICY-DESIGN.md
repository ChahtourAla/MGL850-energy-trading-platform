# B24-A1 spending-policy design

B23 baseline: `89f8c6855df6ecfe13103325894826aff5b4a258`. B24-A1 is limited to a marked local disposable PostgreSQL database and synthetic deployment/consent fixtures. No live B23 Hedera address is recorded. PostgreSQL owner and migration administrator are trusted; role restrictions do not defend against them.

`b24_evaluator` is a distinct authenticated identity. The B22-A historical-read guard admits it only for read-only inspection, and B22-B exposes its existing reconciliation function. The B24 service requires the evaluator session identity and marker, verifies a completed market, archived ingestion and roster, one finalized authoritative plan, all allocation identities and statuses, V1 full-buyer accounting and stored projections. It does not rerun Python or compare current user profiles. The complete allocation list comes from the authoritative database query; B21 IDs, the B23 helper and B22 reconciliation produce the commitment. The underlying simulator's provenance does not prove physical electricity flow or accurate metering.

`B24Deployment`, `B24SpendingPolicy` and `B24AuthorizationReservation` are added by a new migration. `b24_approver` controls fixture deployments and simulated policies; triggers overwrite approval principals with `session_user`, and disallow edits except retirement/revocation. Deployment checks accept only the local chain-31337 fixture and compare stored fingerprints against the compiled B23 artifact, not a live network. The evaluator cannot approve policies, mutate historical records, read wallet secret columns, create evidence, modify schema or disable triggers. Grant scripts require separately provisioned non-owner roles. The fixture addresses are illustrative test identities, not deployed Hedera contracts.

An allocation-level unique constraint prevents reservation across deployments; a deployment/buyer/nonce unique constraint prevents nonce reuse. The service holds a buyer/deployment advisory lock and policy/deployment row locks in a short transaction. The trigger rechecks authoritative allocation, snapshot, policy, deployment, exact amount, deadline and budget under the policy lock. Reserved amounts remain counted; no automatic release or confirmed-spend accounting is implemented. Financial reservation rows are immutable. Identical retries independently rebuild the unsigned EIP-712 digest and compare stored terms; conflicting policy or deployment retries fail. A caller-supplied policy object, allocation list, amount, or commitment is never accepted by the service.

This remains a prototype: the evaluator database role can insert reservation rows and PostgreSQL does not recompute EVM Keccak settlement IDs or full plan commitments. The service performs those checks, and any future B24-B signer must independently reconstruct and revalidate all terms and policy before signing; it must not trust a stored reservation alone. Database uniqueness is not exactly-once blockchain settlement. B24-B must design genuine authenticated consent, keys, signing and revocation; B24-C must handle allowance and controlled funding; B25 must journal uncertain submissions and verify actual receipts/events before payment projection.
