# B23 direct settlement design

This is an offline prototype for a separate direct-payment contract. Legacy escrow and its obligations remain untouched. The contract calls SafeERC20 `transferFrom` directly from buyer to seller after EIP-712 authorization; it never mints or deliberately holds the payment. The buyer must both sign the exact typed terms and grant the contract allowance. A custodial signature is generated by a platform-held key and is not independent evidence of human consent. Key compromise remains a major risk.

B22 determines the authoritative PostgreSQL allocation plan; the chain cannot query or independently validate that decision or physical energy delivery. The first successful payment pins a market's submitted plan commitment and snapshot hash. The contract does not prove allocation membership in the commitment or verify a B22 plan: B24/B25 must reconstruct and validate the full plan before signing or submitting. Incorrectly authorized first payments can pin a wrong plan permanently. No reset is provided. An operator cannot debit approved funds without a valid buyer signature, but compromised admin keys can grant operational roles and compromised buyer keys can authorize loss.

`planCommitment` v1 is Keccak-256 of Solidity `abi.encode(bytes32 domain,bytes32 marketId,bytes32 snapshotHash,bytes32 manifestHash,bytes32[] allocationIds)`; domain is `keccak256(utf8('mgl850:b23:plan:1'))`. IDs are unique and sorted lowercase bytewise; the commitment covers the B21 allocation IDs, not solely the B22 matching-input manifest. Off-chain validation must prove completeness, authoritative status and each ID's actual terms. The contract itself does not verify a membership proof.

B21 market/snapshot/allocation/intent IDs keep their SHA-256 definitions. The B23 settlement ID is the EIP-712 digest of the Settlement structure including token, market, snapshot, plan, allocation, parties, Wh, price, amount, hashed UTF-8 policy versions, nonce and deadline. EIP-712 adds chain ID and deployment address. Used settlement digests, buyer nonces and allocation IDs are guarded inside one deployment. A different deployment has independent storage: cross-deployment duplicate payment requires B24/B25 deployment policy and journaling. Signature domain separation prevents reuse of the old signature, not newly signed payments on a different deployment.

Amounts use positive PostgreSQL BIGINT-bounded quantity, price and payment; product fits uint256 because operands are at most 2^63-1. The contract requires exact division by 1000 and rejects zero/dust. Effects precede SafeERC20; revert rolls back nonce, paid IDs and plan pin. The contract checks both buyer debit and seller credit against the amount, and guards reentrancy. Balance queries and exact deltas do not make an arbitrary malicious token trustworthy; constructor code/decimals checks are not token identity verification. Future deployment must independently verify the actual TEUSD code and immutable address.

Roles are distinct at construction: admin grants/revokes roles, operator submits signatures, pauser pauses/unpauses. Admin can later change memberships. The contract does not support ERC-1271. B24 must design real buyer authority, approvals, limits, key management and revocation; B25 must persist attempts, reconcile uncertain outcomes and verify chain, contract, token, receipt, event and terms before crediting. No live chain action is implemented here.

Before any future deployment, separately review compiler artifact and EVM compatibility, network chain ID 296, TEUSD address recorded in `packages/shared/contracts/deployments/hedera-testnet.json`, independently verified code and six decimals, three intended role addresses, signer authority, deployment gas/HBAR and ABI; distinguish the instance from the legacy escrow. No deployment address is written during B23.
