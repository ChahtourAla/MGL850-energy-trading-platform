## B22-A disposable ingestion prototype

`apps/backend/scripts/b22a-ingestion.mjs` exposes `ingestLocalHour(client, timeSlot, {execute:true})`. It accepts neither uploaded energy records nor a caller-supplied roster. It resolves the latest immutable `B22HourlyRegistry` revision for the hour, compares each frozen profile and public wallet with database users, invokes the repository's local Python generator outside any database transaction, and then rechecks the roster inside a transaction serialized by market and roster advisory locks. It inserts a pending V2 snapshot and measurements, checks the database's canonical measurement hash, finalizes through the B21 triggers, and inserts an immutable `B22Ingestion` audit record including the generated payload. Identical retries reconcile archived content and hashes without reading current profiles or running the generator. An explicit correction requires a new roster revision and an authoritative plan blocks it. All writes are restricted to the loopback `b22_disposable_review` database with its test marker. No migration is applied automatically by the ingestion operation.

The disposable harness `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/b22-run-postgres-tests.ps1` creates and removes only its own labeled PostgreSQL container. Its synthetic administrator inserts the frozen hourly roster; there is no public roster-management endpoint. The harness tests seven participants, concurrent identical execution, injected transaction rollback, immutable finalized measurements and roster, a separately committed correction, and absence of allocation plans and settlement intents. The earlier B09 verifier does not promote old imports to authorized B22 data. A local simulator's provenance and content hash do not attest physical meter readings or electricity delivery.

`tools/b22a-runtime-grants.sql` describes grants for separately provisioned `b22_roster` and `b22_ingest` login identities. The roster role can insert approved rosters but cannot write snapshots or ingestion audit; the ingestion role can read rosters and write snapshots/audit but cannot approve rosters. Triggers replace caller-supplied principal fields with `session_user`. The disposable harness authenticates both logins independently and checks denied operations; the owner creates schema and test fixtures. A narrow security-definer function locks participant profiles without granting runtime profile updates. These controls do not defend against a malicious owner or superuser; production role provisioning and auditing are not implemented.

`inspectHistoricalHour` reports absent, finalized or quarantined records, including incomplete draft status, measurement count and missing provenance. Unknown drafts are never promoted or deleted. The disposable integration harness coordinates two incompatible service requests across approved roster revisions and verifies that the stale one fails. It also pauses the real service before finalization, checks an immediate insertion against the invisible draft, then holds a second insertion at an observed test-only database lock until commit and verifies the B21 rejection and final measurement set, hash and audit. A test-only subprocess substitution verifies that archived replay never invokes the generator and that a reported unsupported generator version is rejected; it is not a real installed-release upgrade. See `B22_TEST_EVIDENCE.md` and `B22_DATABASE_INVARIANTS.md`.

**Not production-ready:** recovery of externally created partial drafts remains deliberately manual. Runtime role provisioning and monitoring require separate controlled operations, and neither a database owner/superuser compromise nor factual accuracy of the simulated input is addressed. The writer must not be used outside the disposable harness. B22-B allocation persistence and future payments, signing and scheduling remain unimplemented.

Future milestones must resolve prefunding and payment authority, permanent dust/aggregation policy, deployment-bound settlement identity, blockchain reconciliation, and scheduling.

## B22-B accepted checkpoint

`apps/backend/scripts/b22b-matching.mjs` reads the archived B22-A ingestion and roster, verifies their finalized history and B21 commitments, executes the existing Python matcher and reference checks outside a transaction, then locks and rechecks the market before writing. Exact positive allocations are inserted planned, validated, and selected by the authoritative plan; position projections and plan selection are committed atomically. No-match plans select zero allocations and record zero matching. Fractional or zero payment results stay in a nonpayable draft with no positions. Matching requires the disposable marker, loopback target, and `b22_match` identity. This prototype is not a production deployment interface.

The B22-B checkpoint is **B22_B_ACCEPTED**: the PostgreSQL tests have demonstrated a nonzero exact-payment authoritative allocation, database rejection of a competing plan, rollback at three transaction boundaries, and Python-free historical replay; different-configuration concurrent service conflict and additional financial-boundary scenarios have been tested; independent review has accepted the checkpoint, and the limitations in the evidence remain. Do not use this disposable prototype to authorize real markets. B22-B creates no settlement intent or blockchain operation.

B22-B accepts only hard-coded backend configuration identities. The alternate `v1-disposable-distance-test` configuration is restricted to the disposable service guard. The manifest commits scoring inputs, so competing configurations have different hashes. Historical reconciliation uses the archived manifest and requires every matched V1 buyer to receive their entire requirement; no Python process is run. A zero-base-unit V1 match is rejected by the Python output contract before persistence.
